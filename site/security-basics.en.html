Security steps you can take

Basic steps recommended by public bodies. You don't have to do everything at once — start with what you can.

  1. Keep your operating system and apps up to date

    Install updates soon after they arrive. Turning on automatic updates helps you not miss them.

    Based on: National Police Agency of Japan: Basic security measures (Japanese) / CISA: Secure Our World tip sheet

  2. Use long passwords, different for each service

    Change default passwords and use long passwords that are hard to guess, a different one for each service. A password manager can store them safely (CISA).

    Based on: National Police Agency of Japan: Basic security measures (Japanese) / CISA: Secure Our World tip sheet

  3. Turn on two-factor (multi-factor) authentication

    This checks it is really you with something in addition to your password, such as a code sent to your phone. Turn it on wherever it is offered.

    Based on: National Police Agency of Japan: Basic security measures (Japanese) / CISA: Secure Our World tip sheet

  4. Be careful with suspicious emails, texts and links

    Do not respond to alarming messages or offers that seem too good to be true; report and delete suspicious ones.

    Based on: CISA: Secure Our World tip sheet

  5. Use security software and keep it updated

    Install antivirus software and keep its definition files up to date.

    Based on: National Police Agency of Japan: Basic security measures (Japanese)

  6. Back up important data

    Keeping copies of photos and documents somewhere else makes it easier to recover after a breakdown or an attack.

    Based on: NCSC: Cyber Aware leaflet for small businesses

If you think you have been affected

  • Japan: National Police Agency cybercrime consultation (Japanese) / IPA (Information-technology Promotion Agency) information security (Japanese) / for shopping or contract trouble, Consumer Hotline 188 (Japanese)
  • United States: CISA guidance for individuals and families (for where to report, please check with your local police or authorities)
  • United Kingdom: Report a scam website to the NCSC
  • Other countries: on the globe, choose the IT security tab and select your country to see its cybersecurity agencies at the bottom.

About this page

This page covers only ways to protect yourself, not how attacks are carried out. The source pages were checked on 25 October 2026. Please check each public body's pages for the latest information. This site never asks for personal information, passwords or money.
