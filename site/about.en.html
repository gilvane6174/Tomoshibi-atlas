About this site

Physical and mental health, and the challenges facing AI (SI), IT and social systems, on one globe.
Operated by Yutaka Yoshioka (吉岡有隆), non-profit

This English page is a translation of the Japanese page. If the two differ, the Japanese page applies to how the site is run, and the original sources apply to the information itself.

Purpose

Tomoshibi Atlas ("Atlas of Lights") is a collection of links that places published information on infectious diseases, mental health and welfare, and medical research on the same globe as security threats to AI (SI), IT and social systems, and information on disasters and crime. By looking at people's health and the troubles of systems and society side by side, it aims to make their connections easier to notice and to help people prepare to live better.

"Lights" are information about the systems that support people's health and daily lives. "Warnings" are information that needs attention: threats to the IT and AI that run those systems, and disasters and crime. On screen, lights carry a small flame mark and warnings a triangular caution mark. For example, if a hospital's or a welfare office's systems are shut down by a cyberattack, the care and support themselves stop. "Research & Response" also includes results achieved with AI and official security, disaster and crime prevention measures.

US Executive Order 14434 (signed on 29 September 2026; text on the White House website) directed US executive departments and agencies to use "SI" (Super Intelligence) in place of "AI" (artificial intelligence) in official correspondence, websites, reports and similar documents, to the extent permitted by law. It applies to the US executive branch and does not change the term worldwide. This site treats information labelled SI as information about AI (marked "AI-related"). Because the abbreviation "SI" has other meanings, entries always explain it, for example "Super Intelligence (SI), the US federal government's term for AI", and only items confirmed from the original's context to be about AI are listed. The site takes no position on which name is correct.

How mental health, infectious diseases and research connect

Countries and international bodies are working on using AI and other tools to detect illness earlier, spot early signs of outbreaks and advance drug research. In Japan, for example, research to help detect depression and other conditions earlier has been selected under a public research programme for implementing AI (examples from FY2021). At the same time, there has been a case in Japan where a hospital's systems were stopped by a cyberattack and it suspended new outpatients (2022).

This site lets you switch between these research developments (Research & Response), the infectious diseases and mental health concerns actually occurring, and the security of the IT that supports them (IT security), while staying on the same region.

Sources: WHO: first global report on AI in health (June 2021) / WHO: launch of the Hub for Pandemic and Epidemic Intelligence (May 2021) / AMED: FY2021 projects on implementing AI in medicine (Japanese) / Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: material on a cyberattack on a medical institution (Dec 2022, Japanese)

Display rules

What the position on the globe means

  • Infectious diseases: shown at the country named in the headline of the report. Reports whose country could not be identified automatically, or that cover the whole world, are listed but not placed on the globe.
  • IT security: shown at the country of the publishing agency. This does not indicate where an attack occurred or where it came from. Because the CISA catalogue is published by a US agency, vulnerabilities in products from any country appear at the United States on the globe.
  • Research & Response, Mental health & Welfare, Crime prevention and Disasters: shown at the country covered by the original source. Automatic headlines (such as NCSC) are shown at the country of the publishing body.
  • Earthquakes: the country is taken from the location text published by USGS (for example "…, Japan"). Earthquakes at sea or elsewhere without a country are listed but not placed on the globe.

What the text size means

Text size is not a rating of severity or danger. It follows these fixed rules:

  • Infectious diseases: reports published within the last 14 days are shown larger (normally listed for up to 120 days)
  • IT security (CISA): vulnerabilities known to be exploited in ransomware are shown larger (normally listed for up to 30 days after being added)
  • IT security (JPCERT/CC alerts): all shown larger (normally listed for up to 30 days). *Not yet enabled; these alerts are not retrieved at present.
  • Research & Response, Mental health & Welfare, Crime prevention and Disasters: set by the operator based on the nature of the source (for example, a major international report)
  • Earthquakes and automatic headlines: those published (or occurring) within 7 days are shown larger (this is not a rating of size or damage)
  • Older items shown only to keep the number of items up (see "Number of items" below) are always shown smaller

Number of items

Each section shows roughly 20 to 50 items, so there is neither too much nor too little. Up to 50 of the newest items within the normal listing period are shown; if there are fewer than 20, slightly older items (up to 2 years for infectious diseases and 1 year for IT security) are added in smaller text, newest first. As new information arrives, the oldest items drop off. Research & Response and Mental health & Welfare show up to 50 of the newest items; Crime prevention and Disasters show up to 50 of the newest items within 90 days (topped up with items from the past 180 days if there are fewer than 20; automatic headlines have a limit per source so that manually added warnings are not pushed out); Earthquakes shows up to 50 of the newest earthquakes of magnitude 5.5 or more in the past 30 days; Today's animals shows 20 each day. If the original sources have not published that many, there may be fewer than 20 (we never make up items to reach a count).

Earlier reports

When a newer report is published for the same topic, area and subject, the older one stays visible, faded and labelled "Earlier report (a newer one exists)", until 7 days after its own publication date, and is then removed. (If the older report was already published more than 7 days earlier, it is removed when the newer one appears.) "Same area" means the newer report covers every country of the older one (for example, an older report on Japan is superseded by a newer report on Japan and Korea, but an older report on Japan and Korea is not superseded by a newer report on Japan alone). Only the latest report appears in the headlines on the globe. Information published more than 30 days ago is labelled as such.

Welfare entries

Challenges in each area's systems are written as the view of the cited report ("According to …"), not as this site's own assessment. They do not rank countries or areas.

Official links by country (support and expert bodies)

When you select a country, the bottom of the details shows links to official sites of public bodies and similar organisations in that country (welfare support, mental health support, cybersecurity agencies, research institutions and official infectious disease information), plus worldwide official information. Only links with a record that AI or the operator actually opened the page and confirmed it as official are shown, each with the date it was checked. Links without such a record, or that could not be confirmed, are not shown until they are confirmed. Whether and how many links a country or region has reflects only how far checking has progressed, not any political judgement. Besides Japan, the United States, the United Kingdom and worldwide information, health, welfare and cybersecurity bodies of other countries are being added step by step; countries not yet checked show "being prepared". Details and contact information can change, so please check the latest information on each linked site.

Borders, country and region names

Borders, country names and region names on the map follow the way maps are generally drawn in Japan, using the "Point of View: Japan" edition of the public Natural Earth map data. Shapes are simplified for the globe, so small islands and fine border details may be inaccurate or not shown. We may not be able to respond to requests to change how borders or names are shown. These representations do not indicate any political position of the operator regarding sovereignty or the status of any country or region.

Day and night

The dark side of the globe is where it is currently night, and the faint orange band is around sunrise or sunset. This is an approximation calculated from the date and time; no external data is used.

About translation

This site can be shown in Japanese or English. To avoid misunderstanding:

  • English headlines retrieved automatically (WHO, CISA) are shown exactly as published in the English display. In the Japanese display, only disease names found in a fixed glossary are shown in Japanese; everything else stays in the original English (no machine translation is used).
  • Information published only in Japanese (such as JPCERT/CC alerts, not yet enabled) is shown in Japanese in the English display, labelled "Japanese only".
  • Summaries added by the operator are prepared in both Japanese and English and checked to have the same meaning before publication.
  • If the Japanese and English text differ, the original source linked from each entry is authoritative.

Safety in operation

Beware of impersonation. This site never asks for personal information, passwords or money. If someone contacts you by email, social media or phone claiming to be this site or its operator (Yutaka Yoshioka), do not respond. As a rule, we do not reply to messages about this site (see "Feedback" below).

We do not link to sites that may be unsafe. Many visitors are in difficulty or anxious, so links go only to official sites of governments, public bodies and international organisations. We do not link to sites that show certificate warnings, do not load, show signs of tampering, pages that ask you to download files, log in or pay, private or personal sites, or short URLs. Organisations with a public role (such as a government-funded crisis line or a national CERT designated by law) may be listed, with how they are run stated in the description. Links are checked automatically every week after publication and removed if a problem is found. When in doubt, we leave a link out. For countries and regions affected by war or conflict, each official site is reviewed individually: public information that helps people living there (health care, livelihood support, safety information and so on) is listed, while pages that include calls for military cooperation or similar content are not.

Preventing misuse: IT security information is limited to what public bodies have already published (such as vulnerabilities confirmed to be exploited, with official remediation guidance). The site does not publish how to carry out attacks, attack code, details usable in attacks (such as IP addresses or file hashes), undisclosed or unpatched vulnerabilities, or system details of individual affected organisations. To prevent misuse, vulnerability reports are added only after the vendor, government or public bodies have published countermeasures (such as a fix or workaround). Information sent to us before countermeasures are published will not be posted (if you find a vulnerability, please report it to the product vendor or, in Japan, to IPA's reporting service). Automated checks block such content before publication. Information retrieved automatically every day (WHO, CISA) is published after these automated checks only, without human review; information added manually is reviewed by the operator before publication.

  • This is a static site with no server, login, input forms or API keys.
  • Automatic updates (infectious diseases and IT security) use only a fixed program and do not pass external text to generative AI.
  • On days when retrieval or checks fail, publication stops and the previous content remains.
  • Changes prepared by AI (manually added information, the link directory and so on) are published only with the operator's approval. The link directory shows only links with a check record, as described above. Information retrieved automatically every day by fixed procedures (WHO, CISA) is published after automated checks only.

About crime prevention, disaster and earthquake information

This site is not a source of information for emergencies. Earthquake and disaster information is retrieved from public data twice a day; preliminary figures may be revised later and may not reflect the latest situation. In a disaster or incident, follow the information and instructions of your national weather agency, local government, police and fire services. In a life-threatening emergency in Japan, call 119 (ambulance/fire) or 110 (police) without hesitation.

Crime and fraud information is limited to warnings and statistics published by public bodies such as the police and consumer authorities. Names of individuals, detailed methods, and information that could identify victims or suspects are not listed.

  • Japan Meteorological Agency: disaster prevention information (warnings, earthquakes, tsunamis, typhoons; in Japanese)
  • Japan Government PR Online: crime prevention (police consultation line #9110 and more; in Japanese)
  • Japan Government PR Online: Consumer Hotline 188 (fraud and contract problems; in Japanese)

If you are struggling
